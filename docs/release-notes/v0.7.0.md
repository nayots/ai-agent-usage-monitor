## Highlights

**The tray icon is readable now.** The percentage gets ten pixels of height instead of eight, and
nothing shares its rows. Where the provider's initials used to sit, there is a three-pixel coloured
flag in one of three positions along the top edge.

**Each tool has its own colour and its own corner.** Clay for Claude Code on the left, teal-green
for Codex in the middle, violet for Cursor on the right. Colour tells you at a glance; position
tells you when colour cannot.

**At the limit the icon floods red and prints nothing.** `100` is three characters squeezed into
sixteen pixels, and it came out smaller than every other reading — smallest exactly when it mattered
most. A square flooded to the edge says it louder.

---

## Why

The previous icon stacked two pieces of type in one sixteen-pixel square: a figure across the top
and the provider's initials across a bar at the bottom. Sixteen pixels does not divide twice. The
initials got five rows of cap height, and at five rows a capital's strokes are narrower than a
pixel — so no pixel of it is ever fully inked, and it renders as a smudge.

It looked fine magnified in a screenshot. It was not fine in the tray, which is the only place it
is ever actually seen.

## What the icon looks like now

One text element, and everything else is a rectangle. Rectangles stay readable down to three
pixels; letters do not.

- **rows 0–3** — the flag: a 3×3 block in the provider's colour, at the left, centre or right.
- **rows 3.5–13.5** — the figure: ten pixels of ink, condensed to fit, nothing overlapping it.
- **rows 14–16** — the gauge: full width, filled from the left, in the usual three usage bands.

The flag and the figure share columns but never rows, so they cannot collide at any size.

## Every state

| | |
|---|---|
| Below 75% | figure in the ink colour, gauge in the accent band |
| 75% and up | same, gauge switches to the high band |
| At the limit | floods in the exhausted colour, no figure; the flag knocks out so its position still names the tool |
| Stale | figure and gauge both grey, the fill lifted a pixel off the left edge |
| Failed | an upright mark, gauge full width in the bad colour, flag still lit |
| Waiting | a short rule and bare track — never a zero |

Stale had no drawing of its own before this release, even though the underlying selector has always
distinguished it.

## Colour is never the only cue

High contrast resolves every fill to one system colour on purpose — that is why exhaustion has
always been carried by a hatch rather than by red. The flag follows the same rule: under high
contrast all three collapse to the window text colour, and the flag's **position** is what still
says which tool is on screen. That is why the flag moves in every theme rather than only in high
contrast: a position nobody has learned is no use at the moment they need it.

The slot is keyed to the provider itself, not to its place in the visible list, so hiding one card
never renumbers the others.

## Sizes

Every size is worked out from its own unit rather than scaled up from sixteen, so the flag and the
gauge land on whole device pixels at 100%, 125%, 150% and 200% display scaling instead of
straddling two.

## Notes

The provider colours evoke each vendor rather than copying them. They are tuned for a three-pixel
block on a taskbar, not lifted as brand hexes, and no vendor's mark or logo appears anywhere in the
application.
