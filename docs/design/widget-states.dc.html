Quota widget — screens and states

Windows 11 desktop widget showing live quota for locally installed AI coding tools. Every screen below is drawn against the fixture data in the brief; nothing is populated that the fixture does not supply. Light and dark are shown together throughout. Layouts are nested stacks and grids with explicit sizing, so they translate to WPF panels directly.

No dash, no zero, no greyed placeholder — an em-dash would read as a value. The bar has no marker because the duration is unknown, which is the baseline bar, not a degraded one. codex at 100% — exhausted. Two things separate it from a merely full bar: the fill is hatched (a tiled brush in WPF, not a colour change), and a second line spells out "Limit reached · resets in 5d 08h" with the countdown promoted to the heaviest type in the row. The marker at 24% stays where it is; the distance between it and the fill edge is the whole point of the row and the UI draws no conclusion from it.
Marker at the extremes. The marker is a 4 px box — a 2 px core with a 1 px surface-coloured gap either side — and its box is inset linearly so that at 0% it sits flush inside the left cap and at 100% flush inside the right. It never overhangs the track and never merges with the end cap. Where marker and fill edge land on the same pixel, the gap keeps them separate; the 3 px overshoot above and below the bar keeps the marker findable at any scaling.

States 8, 11, 12 · Fixtures C, D, E, F and stale data

Card-level states at widget width. All four cards sit in the same list as Fixtures A and B without changing the widget's structure.

Unavailable (C) states that the only mechanism stopped returning data and that no fallback exists. It keeps the triangle and the word, offers diagnostics, and shows no bars at all — there is nothing to show, and a greyed bar would imply there was.
Zero windows (E) is not an error and not zero usage. It says what happened — the provider reported no windows — and stops. No bar is drawn, because a 0% bar would be a fabricated value.
Six windows (F) proves the card scales by stacking. The card never truncates: rows keep rendering and the widget grows to a 520 px ceiling, after which the provider list — not any individual card — scrolls. Compact mode is where collapsing begins, at three rows per card.
Stale keeps the last known values at secondary ink (5.1:1 light, 5.8:1 dark — de-emphasised, still comfortably readable), swaps the accent bar fill for neutral grey so a stale bar cannot be mistaken for a live one, and states the age of the last successful update with the refresh action beside it.

Three steps, not a gradient, and the same three in both themes. Applied to every bar in the app — tray rules and widget quota rows alike — behind the Colour bars by usage setting. Off, every bar returns to the single accent and the exhausted red. Bar length and the written percentage already carry the value, so tone is redundant reinforcement rather than the sole signal, which is what keeps it inside the no-colour-only rule. It does cost the "at most one accent hue" restraint rule; that trade is worth confirming.
B is the recommendation. Windows already stacks one icon per app in the overflow, and a single digit pair cannot honestly stand for two providers at once. One icon per provider means the number always belongs to something nameable, and Codex's weekly window gets a full-width rule instead of a third of one.
The number is the highest percentage the provider reported, not an average and not a chosen window — averaging separate allowances would be an invented value. The rules underneath are every window in reported order, so the number always has a rule that matches it.
100% never renders as digits. Three glyphs do not fit in a 16 px cell at a legible size, and a truncated "10" would be a wrong number. At 100% with rate-limit-reached the digits are replaced by the limit-reached disc, which is the state that matters more than the figure.
Every variant sums to exactly 16 px at 1× with integer parts: variant B is 8 px of digits over three 2 px rules with 1 px gaps; variant A drops the digits to 7 px to buy the 2 px group gap above the Codex rule. Nothing is shrunk to fit. The icon is authored as vector and Windows renders it at 20, 24 and 32 px for 125%, 150% and 200% scaling, so the 16 px case is the floor, not the norm. In high-contrast mode the fills become Highlight on Control and the digits WindowText. The limit-reached disc sits flush in the bottom-right corner, where it can only clip the tail of the lowest rule — the exhausted one, whose fill runs the full width anyway.
The tooltip states reset times as countdowns only. Absolute clock times would have to be computed and are not in the provider payload, and nothing in the tooltip projects forward.
